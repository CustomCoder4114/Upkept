# Upkept — Product Plan

Nov 21, 2026 · @Superior1\_

## What Upkept is

Upkept is one app for everything in your life that can expire, and it doesn't just remind you: it helps you get each renewal, claim or cancellation done.

**One-line pitch:** *Snap any document. Upkept reads the dates, keeps it safe, reminds you in time, and shows you exactly how to renew it.*

**The twist:** other apps stop at "Your passport expires in 30 days." Upkept says: "Your passport expires in 6 months. Many countries refuse entry with less than 6 months left. Here's how to renew it in your country: what to bring, the cost, how long it takes, and the official link."

**What it replaces:** document scanners, document vaults, expiry trackers, subscription trackers, bill reminders, warranty and receipt apps, and the notes people keep for "how do I renew this?"

**Who it's for:** every adult, anywhere in the world, plus the family members they look after (children, partners, elderly parents).

**Platforms:** Android, iPhone and web from the start. Most competitors are iPhone-only.

**Working name:** Upkept. Consumer Reports already publishes a home-maintenance app called Upkept, so the name must be checked with a trademark search before public launch, or adjusted (for example "Upkept Life").

## The problem

Almost everything an adult owns or signs up for has a date attached, and people find out it has passed only when it costs them.

- **Missed dates cost money:** late fines on vehicle papers, a lapsed insurance policy just before an accident, a warranty that ended a week before the phone broke, a subscription that renewed for another year.
- **Missed dates cost opportunities:** a trip cancelled because the passport has under 6 months left, a job start delayed by an expired work permit or licence.
- **The papers are scattered:** in drawers, email inboxes, phone galleries, WhatsApp chats and wallets. When something is lost or stolen, there is no copy.
- **Knowing the date isn't enough:** people still don't know how to renew, what to bring, what it costs, or how long it takes, so they put it off.
- **Families multiply the load:** one person usually tracks everything for children, a partner and ageing parents, in their head.

Calendars remember meetings. Nothing remembers the passport, the car insurance, the laptop warranty and the gym membership together, and nothing helps you act on them.

## The gap

No app we found combines every kind of expiring item in one place, and none helps the user actually act on a reminder.

1. **Nobody does the "get it done" step.** Every app stops at "X expires soon". None gives country-specific renewal steps, warranty claim help, cancellation steps or lost-document recovery.
2. **Everything is split across apps.** Documents live in one app, subscriptions in another, warranties in a third. Users must run three or four apps to cover their life.
3. **Most are built for iPhone users in rich countries.** Most of the world uses Android and lives outside the US and Europe, where renewal rules and offices differ.
4. **Families are an afterthought.** Few apps manage a child's passport, a parent's medicines and a partner's licence with proper sharing and emergency access.
5. **Subscription tracking needs your bank login.** The big subscription apps read your bank account. Many people won't hand that over.
6. **No reason to come back.** Expiry dates are rare, so simple trackers get opened once and forgotten until a notification arrives.

Upkept fills all six.

## Every feature

Upkept has nine feature areas; the first four are the core, and "Get it done" is what makes it different.

### 1. Capture: getting things in

- **Snap a photo** of any document, card, receipt or letter. Upkept reads the type, issuer, number, dates and amount.
- **Forward emails** (receipts, policies, booking and renewal emails) to a personal address like `name@in.upkept.app`; they are added automatically.
- **Import** PDFs, screenshots and photos from the gallery or files.
- **Add by voice or typing:** "My car insurance ends 3 March."
- **Confirm screen:** the user always checks what was read and taps Confirm or fixes a field, so a wrong date never slips through.
- **Ready-made templates** for common items (passport, driving licence, gym membership) when there's nothing to scan.

### 2. Vault: safe storage

- Encrypted storage, opened with PIN, fingerprint or face.
- Automatic folders: Identity, Travel, Vehicles, Home, Insurance, Health, Money, Work, School, Purchases, Digital.
- Plain-language search: "my laptop receipt", "what expires this month".
- Offline copies of chosen documents, so they open at an airport or border with no internet.
- Every item keeps its history: old versions after a renewal, notes and attachments.

### 3. Timeline: everything that can expire

- One timeline: overdue, this week, this month, later this year.
- Colour by urgency: green (safe), amber (coming up), red (urgent or expired).
- Days-left countdown on every item and a home-screen widget.
- Covers: identity and travel papers, vehicles, home and lease, insurance, health (medicines, prescriptions, vaccines, check-ups), purchases (warranties, return windows), work and education (licences, certificates, fees), digital (domains, hosting, software, phone plans), money (subscriptions, cards, loans, tax dates) and personal (tickets, vouchers, gift cards, loyalty points).

### 4. Reminders that adapt

- Timing set per item type: passport at 6 months, 3 months, 1 month and 1 week; car insurance at 30, 7 and 1 day; a subscription 3 days before it charges; a return window 2 days before it closes.
- Channels: phone notification and email first; WhatsApp and SMS later.
- Escalation: an ignored reminder comes back sooner and more clearly.
- Snooze, "already done" and "not renewing" buttons on every reminder.
- A weekly "Due soon" summary and a monthly "All clear" report.

### 5. Get it done (the main difference)

- **Renewal guides by country and item:** steps, documents to bring, cost, processing time, official link and a "last checked" date.
- **Checklists** the user ticks off as they go.
- **Warranty claim helper:** pulls up the receipt and warranty and shows the claim steps and contact.
- **Cancel helper:** step-by-step cancellation instructions for each service, plus a ready-written cancellation email.
- **Lost or stolen document help:** the stored copy plus the steps to report and replace it.
- **After renewing:** snap the new document and the item resets with its new date; no retyping.

### 6. Money view

- Money calendar: everything that will charge the user in the coming months.
- Yearly cost of subscriptions, insurance and renewals, by category.
- "You haven't used this in 2 months" nudges for subscriptions.
- Works from documents and emails; no bank login needed.

### 7. Family and sharing

- Profiles for children, a partner or parents, each with their own items and reminders.
- Choose who sees what: share a single item, a folder or a whole profile.
- Safe one-off sharing: send an ID copy to a landlord with a link that expires.
- Emergency access: a trusted person can open chosen documents if something happens to the user, after a waiting period the user sets.

### 8. Travel check

- Enter a destination and dates; Upkept checks passport validity (including 6-month rules), visa needs, travel insurance and driving permits for everyone on the trip.
- Result: a simple "Ready" or "Not ready" with what to fix and by when.

### 9. Life in Order score and rewards

- A score from 0 to 100 that rises as items are added, kept valid and renewed on time.
- Badges: "Nothing expired for a year", "Fully covered", "Family organiser", "Travel ready".
- Streaks for clearing reminders on time.
- This gives people a reason to open Upkept between rare expiry dates.

## How a user uses Upkept

Every item follows the same loop (add, read, track, remind, get it done), and a new user should feel the value within 2 minutes.

### First time: onboarding in 2 minutes

1. Sign up with phone number, email or Google/Apple account.
2. Choose country, language and who it's for: "Just me" or "Me and my family".
3. Upkept asks: "Let's start with your 3 most important things." It suggests ID, passport and phone plan (adjusted by country).
4. The user snaps them; each is read, confirmed and placed on the timeline.
5. The home screen shows the first timeline and a Life in Order score (for example 35/100), with suggestions to raise it: "Add your car insurance (+10)".
6. Notifications permission is asked here, with a clear reason: "So we can warn you before anything expires."

### Journey 1: Amina's car insurance

1. Amina photographs her insurance certificate.
2. Upkept fills in: car insurance, Jubilee, policy number, ends 3 March 2027, $420 a year. She taps Confirm.
3. The certificate goes into her vault; "Car insurance, 102 days" appears on her timeline in green.
4. On 1 February she gets a reminder; it turns amber. On 24 February a second, more urgent one.
5. She opens it and sees: renew steps, what to have ready, her insurer's link, and "Last year you paid $420; compare quotes before renewing."
6. After renewing, she snaps the new certificate. The item resets to 3 March 2028.

### Journey 2: Brian's trip abroad

1. Brian adds a trip: London, 14 to 28 June.
2. Travel check finds his passport expires in October, under the 6-month rule for some destinations, and his visa is missing.
3. Result: "Not ready: renew passport by 1 May, apply for visa by 15 May." Each item links to its country guide and checklist.
4. He ticks off the steps; the check turns "Ready".

### Journey 3: a broken phone under warranty

1. Grace's phone screen stops working. She searches "phone warranty".
2. Upkept shows the receipt from 10 months ago and "Warranty ends in 2 months."
3. The claim helper lists the steps, the shop's contact and what to bring. She gets it repaired free.

### Journey 4: subscription clean-up

1. Kevin forwards his subscription emails to his Upkept address.
2. The money view shows 7 subscriptions costing $46 a month, and flags two he hasn't used in 3 months.
3. He taps "Cancel help" on one; Upkept shows the steps and a ready cancellation email.

### Journey 5: looking after a parent

1. Joyce creates a profile for her mother and adds her medical cover, ID and monthly prescription.
2. Her brother is invited to the profile; both get the reminders.
3. Joyce sets emergency access so her brother can open the documents if needed.

### Everyday use

- Most days: nothing to do. Upkept works quietly in the background.
- Weekly: a 10-second "Due soon" summary.
- When something new arrives (a receipt, a new card, a policy): snap or forward it.
- When a reminder comes: open it, follow the guide, snap the new document.

## How it works behind the scenes

The website and the mobile app talk to one FastAPI backend; a background worker sends reminders even when nobody opens the app.

&#91;embedded content: Upkept architecture · 2 apps, 1 backend, 5 services\]

Only the backend touches the database, files and AI; the worker (highlighted) is the heart of Upkept.

**Stack:** Next.js website, React Native + Expo app (TypeScript shared between both, with an API client generated from FastAPI), FastAPI, PostgreSQL, Redis with Celery or Arq for the worker, S3-compatible file storage (AWS S3, Cloudflare R2 or Backblaze), Expo push notifications, an email service (Postmark, Resend or Amazon SES), and later Twilio for WhatsApp and SMS.

**How documents are read (AI):**

1. The phone reads text first, free and offline (Google ML Kit on Android, Apple Vision on iPhone).
2. If needed, the backend sends the text or image to an AI API (Claude, GPT or Gemini, or a document service like Google Document AI, AWS Textract or Azure Document Intelligence), which returns type, issuer, number, dates and amount.
3. The user confirms or fixes each field.

All AI calls go through one backend function, `read_document()`, so the provider can be swapped without touching the rest of the app. We use providers that don't train on our data, hide ID numbers before sending where possible, and set a monthly spending limit. Building our own model waits until there are millions of users.

**Reminders:** the worker runs every few minutes, finds items whose reminder time has come, sends the push notification and email, and records it. Ignored reminders are scheduled again, sooner.

**Security:** files encrypted at rest; sensitive fields (passport and ID numbers) encrypted in the database; app lock with PIN or face; expiring share links; full account and data deletion on request.

**Offline:** chosen documents are kept in a small encrypted database on the phone (SQLite), so they open without internet.

**Build order:** backend first, then the website (guides start bringing users), then the mobile app.

## Business model and costs

Upkept is free to start, earns from a Premium subscription, and stays ad-free because users trust it with their documents.

| Plan | Price (to test) | What's included |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free | $0 | Unlimited items typed in, 10 AI scans a month, basic reminders, 1 profile, country guides |
| Premium | About $2–4 a month, adjusted by country | Unlimited scans, email forwarding, family profiles, travel check, money view, more storage, WhatsApp/SMS reminders |
| Family | Slightly more than Premium | Premium for up to 6 people, shared profiles, emergency access |

**Later income (only if it helps users):** partner offers at renewal time, such as comparing insurance quotes, clearly labelled. No selling of user data, ever.

**Main running costs:**

- AI document reading: pay per scan, a small amount each; kept low by reading text on the phone first and calling the API only when needed.
- File storage for documents.
- Servers, database and background worker.
- Email, push, and later WhatsApp/SMS messages.
- Keeping country guides accurate.

Start-up spend can stay near zero while building: free tiers and starter credits from the AI and hosting providers cover testing. Check each provider's current prices before choosing.

## Growth plan

Free country renewal guides on the website are the main engine: people searching "how to renew X in Y" land on Upkept and sign up.

1. **Country guide pages (search traffic).** One public page per item per country: "Renew a passport in the Philippines", "Renew a driving licence in Kenya". Each ends with "Get reminded next time: track it in Upkept." Built with Next.js so search engines rank them; organised per country like Eschool's course folders.
2. **Panic moments.** Marketing speaks to the moment people search for help: a lost passport, a late fine, a surprise charge, a broken phone. "Never pay a late fine again."
3. **Family invites.** Every family member added can be invited to their own account; shared profiles bring in new users.
4. **Safe sharing links.** When a user shares an ID copy or a checklist, the link page shows "Shared with Upkept" and a sign-up button.
5. **Android and web first.** Most competitors are iPhone-only, leaving most of the world's phone users unserved.
6. **Retention loops.** The weekly "Due soon" summary, the money calendar, the monthly "All clear" report and the Life in Order score bring people back between expiry dates.
7. **Seasonal pushes.** New year ("start the year with nothing expired"), travel seasons, school terms and tax season.
8. **Later partners.** Insurers, driving schools, travel agents, employers' HR teams and Eschool parents, where it truly helps users.

**Numbers to watch:** sign-ups, items added in the first week (target: 5 or more), reminders acted on, weekly active users, and free-to-Premium conversion.

## Risks and how we handle them

The biggest risk is trust: Upkept stores passports and IDs, so security has to be right from day one.

| Risk | Why it matters | How we handle it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Data leak | Passport and ID copies are very sensitive | Encrypt files and sensitive fields, strong login, no ads, no data selling, security review before launch |
| Wrong date read by AI | A missed renewal breaks trust | User always confirms; sanity checks (end date after start date); easy edit |
| Outdated guides | Renewal rules and fees change | Official source and "last checked" date on every guide; scheduled reviews; users can report changes |
| People forget the app | Expiry dates are rare | Weekly summary, money calendar, score and badges |
| Reminders not delivered | Phones block or delay notifications | Email as backup, later WhatsApp/SMS; a test reminder at sign-up |
| Crowded "scan and remind" market | Many small apps already exist | Lead with "Get it done", Android and web, worldwide guides, families |
| Name conflict | Consumer Reports owns an app called Upkept | Trademark search before launch; adjust the name if needed |
| Privacy laws differ by country | Rules on storing ID documents vary | Clear privacy policy, data deletion on request, storage region choices as we grow |
| AI costs grow | Every scan costs a little | Phone reads text first; free-plan scan limit; Premium covers the cost |
